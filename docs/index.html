01 The framework

One performance.
Two perspectives.

DRAME-EvalBench pairs bilingual human judgments with complementary evaluation protocols. DRAME-Eval learns to estimate those judgments from speech and textual context.

Does the voice
fit the role?

Begin with a role and scene. Generate speech, then ask human raters whether it sounds natural, clear, and appropriate for the character.

  1. 01Role & sceneSet the character
  2. 02Generated speechPerform the role
  3. 03Human judgmentsEvaluate the fit

What is scored Audio quality, human likeness, and appropriateness, with a separate content-pass check.

Does the delivery
fit its context?

Begin with a human recording and reconstruct its character and scene. Assess fine-grained delivery and role fit, using contrastive mismatches to probe evaluator failures.

  1. 01Human recordingAnchor the context
  2. 02Context & mismatchesProbe role, scene, delivery
  3. 03Human judgmentsAssess ten dimensions

What is scored Ten delivery and role-fit dimensions. The basic set mixes real and TTS audio; the held-out authentic-speech stress test uses human speech only.

02 The findings

The synthetic-to-authentic gap

Within-speaker correlation on authentic speech is only +0.029, compared with matched human single-rater agreement of +0.34. Strong benchmark alignment does not establish reliable discrimination of acting differences among human performances.

Values are mean Pearson correlations under masking for unavailable labels. Pooled bilingual correlations can reflect between-language effects; see the paper for per-language results and uncertainty. The authentic-speech pool contains 1,080 adaptation and 1,084 held-out test records; the entire pool is not a test set.
